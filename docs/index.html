Quantum theory predicts music that cannot compose.

We classify when it does. Quantum transition probabilities do not multiply like classical probabilities: checking a process step by step can change its final predictions.

The musical version

A score can lose its past.

A quantum passage may play predictably from the beginning yet become unpredictable when the conductor enters midway through it.

01 · The paradox

One process. Two ways of asking what happened.

Observe only the endpoint, or check every intermediate step. Classically the two descriptions compose. Quantum mechanically they need not agree.

I

The problem

Intermediate measurements erase interference, so the probabilities inferred step by step can differ from the final outcome distribution.

II

The classification

Agreement on every contiguous interval occurs exactly when every operation is a phase-dressed permutation—the quantum counterpart of a deterministic reversible process.

III

The obstruction

We quantify the failure, prove sharp bounds, and show that predicting it is computationally as hard as simulating quantum computation itself.

Classical composition asks for 𝓑(VU) = 𝓑(V)𝓑(U) Quantum interference can break the equality.

Hear composition—and its failure.
